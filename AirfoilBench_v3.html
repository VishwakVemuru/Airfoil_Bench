Cl and the flow field come from a live lumped-vortex/source panel solve with an unsteady discrete-vortex wake and ground-effect image vortices. Cd, L/D and stall angle are empirical estimates (turbulent flat-plate skin friction + simplified stall/flap correlations) for teaching intuition — not substitutes for XFOIL or tunnel data. Lift/span assumes a 1 m chord. Ground proximity is modeled as a plane parallel to the chord, not true horizontal ground.

Geometry: exact NACA 4-digit and 5-digit closed-form equations generate the camber line and thickness envelope. A flap is modeled as a rigid rotation of the mean camber line aft of a hinge — the classic thin-airfoil flap treatment.

Lift & pressure: a lumped-vortex/source panel method (validated against thin-airfoil theory) solves for the bound circulation. Cp is sampled from the resulting velocity field just off the surface.

Wake: discrete vortices shed periodically from the trailing edge (or an estimated separation point past stall), alternating in sign like a von Kármán street, then advected by the combined field of every other vortex — a real (if simplified) unsteady vortex method, not a scripted animation.

Ground effect: handled by the classic method of images — a mirrored set of vortices/sources below a ground plane enforces the zero-flow boundary there, which is exactly why real aircraft feel a lift boost close to the runway.

What's approximate: Cd, stall angle and L/D come from empirical correlations (turbulent flat-plate skin friction, Reynolds scaling), not a boundary-layer solve — treat them as reasonable teaching estimates, not XFOIL-grade numbers.
